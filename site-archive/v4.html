Before you download — the things I'd ask

Fair
questions.

Running a stranger's installer is a real risk and worth being careful about. Here is everything I would want to know first, answered straight.

Can I read the source before I run it?

Yes, and you don't have to run it to do so. Pixal installs as plain Python — nothing compiled, nothing obfuscated — and the installer payload is a git archive of the repository, so the code on your disk after setup is the code. The full source is going public as a repository, with that exact tree attached to each release as a source zip carrying its own sha256. Unpacking the .exe itself won't work today — it's Inno Setup 6.4, which neither 7-Zip nor innoextract 1.9 can open — so the zip is the route. Read it, change it for yourself; the licence asks only that you don't pass it on.

Does anything leave my machine?

By default, nothing. Pixal listens on 127.0.0.1; chats, characters, settings and history stay in the Pixal folder; renders stay under output\pixal_dm. Two exceptions, both yours to switch on: an API chat brain receives the messages you send it, and LAN access puts the app on your own network behind an access key.

Is it free? Is anything metered?

Free. No account, no queue, $0.00 a picture. The only thing that can cost money is optional: point the chat brain at an API provider and that provider bills your key, under your account with them. The local brain has neither.

Will it touch the ComfyUI I already run?

It installs beside it, never inside it, and refuses to install over one. It starts your existing install with your existing launcher and flags, adds only the node packs it needs, and leaves a ComfyUI carrying local edits alone. Uninstall it and nothing about your setup has changed.

Does it free VRAM between the chat brain and ComfyUI?

Yes, and you decide how. The chat brain can stay resident for instant replies, unload after every reply, or run on the CPU — one setting. Settings → Compute has free VRAM and free brain for eviction on demand, and a render that runs out of card clears it and retries a size down rather than dying.

Can it use the models and LoRAs I already have?

Point Pixal at your existing folders and rescan — nothing is moved or copied. Your checkpoints appear in the model pill if they belong to a family Pixal has a profile for (Krea 2, Z-Image and Anima today), and your LoRAs join the chain, filtered to the ones the selected model can actually take.

Do I need to know ComfyUI?

No. Pixal builds and queues the graphs; you never see a node. If you want the full editor it is one setting away, or at the ComfyUI address any time — but nothing in the normal flow asks for it.

What does it need?

Windows 11 x64 and an NVIDIA card on driver 580 or newer. 24 GB and up runs everything, video included; 16 GB runs every still model and LTX video; 8–16 GB wants quantized builds. Under the comfortable tier things still run — they stream weights and slow down rather than refuse.

Mac? Linux?

Windows is the supported target today. The Linux port is written but unreleased — LINUX.md is the runbook and makes no promises. There is no Mac build.

Can I sell what I make with it?

The pictures are yours; the licence claims no rights in your output, and you may run Pixal commercially. Three components in the box carry non-commercial terms of their own — the Anima model, FLUX.2 Klein and the PiD upscaler — and those ride on the checkpoint, not on your pictures.

Longer answers — install, settings, troubleshooting, the full FAQ — live in the manual, which also ships in the box.
